# Evidence-Backed Audit: CanLII RefLex, Westlaw KeyCite, LexisNexis Shepard's

**Purpose:** determine whether a Canadian academic citation-extraction pipeline **duplicates or complements** the three named citators.
**Prepared:** as a research audit. Every claim below is tied to a URL and a short verbatim quote. Items I could not verify are marked **UNVERIFIED** or **NOT FOUND**. No speculation is offered as fact.

## 1. CanLII RefLex

Primary sources: [RefLex Citator](http://www.canlii.org/en/info/reflex.html) · [CanLII FAQ](https://www.canlii.org/info/faq.html) · [CanLII Terms of Use](https://www.canlii.org/info/terms.html) · [Scope of CanLII's Databases](https://www.canlii.org/collections) · [CanLII blog, 2020-01-23](https://blog.canlii.org/2020/01/23/introducing-canliis-latest-research-tools-decision-highlights-and-a-powerful-new-noteup-%e2%9c%a8/) · [CanLII blog, 2020-11-17](https://blog.canlii.org/2020/11/17/a-new-wave-of-improvements-on-canlii-%f0%9f%8c%8a/) · [CanLII blog, 2015-03-17](https://blog.canlii.org/2015/03/17/canlii-professional-users-news/) · [CanLII blog, 2024-11-04](https://blog.canlii.org/2024/11/04/an-update-concerning-unauthorized-collection-and-use-of-canlii-data/) · [CanLII REST API docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/canlii/API_documentation/master/EN.md) · [Lexum, "Reflex – Bridging Open Access with a Legacy Legal Information System"](https://lexum.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/2005-reflex-lexum.pdf)

### 1a. RefLex specifics asked about explicitly

| Sub-question | Verified answer |
|---|---|
| **How are citations harvested — human or program?** | **Program, with a legacy human-compiled core.** *"most citations added to RefLex are harvested and processed by computer programs"*; historically CanLII editors *"compiled hundreds of thousands of citations to main Canadian case law reports"*; a program *"analyses each decision to identify all citations"* using *"regular expressions"*; editors intervene only on unresolved pairs and on *"over 100 of the most important sources of citations"*. (reflex.html; Lexum 2005; blog 2015-03-17) |
| **How are parallel citations used to infer the same decision?** | *"RefLex uses this data to find parallel citations associated with a cited decision. Once a new citation from a parallel citation set is known, RefLex infers that the other parallel citations of the same set are related to the same decision. RefLex thus 'learns' new citations over time and continues to improve."* (reflex.html) Reflex 3 extended the set beyond print reporters to *"online services like Quicklaw, Westlaw Canada, Jurisprudence Express or Azimut"*. (blog 2015-03-17) |
| **What does the Treatment tab show?** | *"Clicking on the 'Treatment' tab will display 3 'subtabs'. The 'Citations' subtab shows a list of cases that cite the decision being consulted in reverse chronological order. The 'Discussions' subtab shows a list of documents (decisions and pieces of commentary) sorted in descending order of 'discussion identity' (blue jalapeño), including documents with 'unfavourable mentions' (yellow jalapeño). The 'Unfavourable mentions' subtab applies a filter to the previous subtab to show only documents that had a yellow jalapeño in the previous subtab."* (reflex.html; same structure described in blog 2020-11-17) |
| **The "jalapeño" indicators** | Blue = intensity of discussion, 0–5 bands, *"the maximum spiciness is 5 jalapeños"*; *"A mention of a case's reference in a footnote will probably not trigger a jalapeño, but several mentions of the same case in a discussed decision will."* Yellow = *"unfavourable mentions"* — *"a (flavourful) heads up to you that they contain those types of markers."* Goal statement: *"the goal of the new noteup feature is to help you identify cases that contain the most extensive discussion of a previous case (whether positive, negative or neutral), not necessarily the case with the most severe criticism."* (blogs 2020-01-23, 2020-11-17) |
| **Reliance on neutral citations (post-1999)** | **Reliance is explicit but partial and one-directional.** *"The widespread use of the Neutral citation standard for case law (1999) in decisions enables RefLex to yield a high rate of recognized cited decisions."* (reflex.html) The mechanism is agnostic about which citation flavour appears: recognition works off *"properly styled citations, while tolerating several common variations"*, and parallel-citation set inference supplies the pre-1999 / non-neutral cases. The Lexum paper notes the design goal that *"the neutral citation appears alone or in first place"* to be counted, and that neutral citation *"is not available for the years prior to 1999"*. **No rate or percentage for neutral-citation dependence is published — NOT FOUND.** |

| 2 | **Treatment taxonomy** — labels, and who assigns them? | **The richest taxonomy of the three; assigned by attorney-editors, with automation upstream.** <br>• Signals: red *"Warning: Negative treatment is indicated"*; orange *"Questioned: Validity questioned by citing references"*; yellow *"Caution: Possible negative treatment indicated"*; blue *"A" – "Neutral: Citing references with analysis available"*; plus *"Cited by: Citation information available"* and, for statutes, a red exclamation (Shepard's Signal Indicators PDF). <br>• Analysis phrases include *"Overruled by / Abrogated by / Superseded by / Unconstitutional by / Void or invalid by / Questioned by / Criticized by / Distinguished by / Explained by / Cited in Dissenting Opinion at / Interpreted or construed by / Followed by"* (Shepard's Signal Indicators PDF). ## 4. Cross-provider summary: what NONE of the three does

### 4.1 Mention-level printed-form statistics — none

No provider publishes, per citing relationship, the individual citation **mentions** with character/paragraph offsets, and none publishes the **printed form** of each mention (e.g. whether the court wrote `[1992] 3 SCR 813` or `1992 CanLII 25 (SCC)` or `Moge`). What exists is coarser:

| Provider | Closest thing it exposes | What is still missing |
|---|---|---|
| RefLex | Per-paragraph citing-document **counts** ("the speech bubble indicates the number of citations") and a 0–5 discussion-intensity band | Offsets, per-mention printed form, raw mention count |
| KeyCite | A first-reference print page in Table of Authorities; a direct-quote boolean; (Canada) a 1–4 depth value *"based upon the number of times the cited case is mentioned"* | The underlying mention count itself; offsets; printed forms |
| Shepard's | First-reference text block; pin cites; multi-label per citing case; a 1–4 algorithmic DoD band | Raw mention count; offsets; printed forms |

**All three record treatment at the level of the *document pair* (citing case → cited case), not the individual mention.** RefLex is the only one that explicitly refuses to characterise the pair (see 1.2 above).

### 4.2 Foreign-citation jurisdiction resolution — none

| Provider | Foreign-court citations to its own cases | Its own cases citing foreign courts | Resolves origin jurisdiction of a cited foreign case? |
|---|---|---|---|
| RefLex | CanLII hosts only Canadian content plus a tiny `FOREP` "Foreign reported decisions" database ("not large"; the collections table row reads `FOREP|Foreign reported decisions|N/A|2023-12-13|17|`) | Detected but unlinked if not in collection: *"the citation may not be known to the system, in which case no link is added"* | **No documented origin-resolution field — UNVERIFIED** |
| KeyCite | Not enumerated in US coverage (West's National Reporter System, US statutes/agencies) | **Canada explicitly includes them:** *"judicial treatments of Canadian and foreign cases discussed in Canadian decisions"*, but only within 1867/1977/1986 coverage bands | **No documented origin-resolution field — UNVERIFIED** |
| Shepard's | Enumeration is US-only (federal, 50 states + DC + PR, USCS, CFR, US agencies); historical source: *"there is no Shepard's service available for United Kingdom material on LEXIS-NEXIS"* | Not enumerated | **No documented origin-resolution field** (user must read the citing block) |

**None provides a citation-level field answering "which legal system/court does this cited authority come from?"** — that inference is left to the reader in all three.

### 4.3 Published error / undercounting ledgers — none

| Provider | Published accuracy/precision/recall figures | Explicit statement about incorrect links or trading recall for precision |
|---|---|---|
| **RefLex** | **None** | **YES — the strongest of the three:** *"This automation may entail a certain rate of incorrect linking but we choose to obtain a high level of reliability even if it means adding fewer links."* Plus FAQ: auto-enhancement errors *"will take time to correct."* |
| **KeyCite** | **None published by TR.** Measured only externally: **38%** of negative references missed/incorrectly described; **11%** of negative labels incorrect (Hellyer ¶¶50, 52) | **NOT FOUND.** Closest is a precision-preserving flagging rule (red flag only when the citing court is same-jurisdiction *and* binding) and the note that a yellow flag appears for treatments *"not yet editorially analysed."* |
| **Shepard's** | **None published by Lexis.** Measured only externally: **33%** of negative references missed/incorrectly described; **12%** of negative labels incorrect (Hellyer ¶¶50, 52) | **NOT FOUND as a trade-off statement.** Explicit *undercounting* admissions do exist: *"not all documents available on LexisNexis have associated Shepard's reports"*; *"Some materials provide limited coverage by design"*; *"If a Shepard's report cannot be retrieved using available citation formats, no report exists."* |

### 4.4 Other things none of them does

1. **No raw mention count is exposed by any of the three.** Each substitutes a banded proxy: jalapeño 0–5 (RefLex), depth of treatment 1–4 (KeyCite, incl. Canada), Depth of Discussion 1–4 bars (Shepard's).
2. **No provider publishes the formula for its depth/discussion score.** RefLex says only *"discussion identity"* / *"discussion intensity"*; KeyCite Canada says it is *"based upon the number of times the cited case is mentioned"* without an algorithm; Shepard's says only *"determined algorithmically."*
3. **No provider offers unconstrained bulk or scraping rights.** CanLII bans *"Bulk or systematic downloading… including via programmatic means"* and litigated in Nov 2024; TR bans *"scrape"*, derivative works, white-labelling, and AI/ML training on API content; Lexis gates APIs behind an approval/pricing conversation.
4. **Only one is a Canadian-coverage citator in its flagship form.** Shepard's documented coverage is US-only; KeyCite's documented Canadian coverage lives in the separate **KeyCite Canada** product (derived from The Canadian Abridgment, per the Westlaw Canada card); RefLex covers Canadian case law and legislation free to the public. Canadian treatment labelling from LexisNexis sits in a *different* product, **QuickCITE**, whose taxonomy is *"Distinguished / Explained / Followed / Followed in Minority Opinion / Cited / Cited in Dissenting Opinion / Mentioned / Not Followed / Questioned"* with signals *"Negative Treatment / Cautionary Treatment / Positive Treatment / Neutral History or Treatment."*
5. **Treatment is a document-pair judgement in all three, and all three (except RefLex, which declines to label at all) can only express one kind of criticism per pair** — except Shepard's, which Hellyer reports can carry both positive and negative labels with pin cites for the same citing case.

### 4.5 Where a Canadian academic trace-extraction pipeline is complementary rather than duplicative

Grounded strictly in the verified gaps above — these are the four things **no** audited citator supplies:

1. **Mention-level printed-form data** (per-mention offsets + the citation string as printed), which none exposes and which the providers' own depth scores only approximate (4.1).
2. **Foreign-citation origin resolution** as a data field (4.2).
3. **A public error/undercounting ledger** for the citator's own output — no provider publishes one, and the two providers with independent measurements both show roughly a third of negative relationships missed or misdescribed while publishing **no** number of their own (4.3).
4. **Unconstrained research reuse** — all three restrict bulk extraction, so any pipeline that needs corpus-scale or span-scale data cannot lawfully substitute a citator for its own extraction (4.4 item 3, §1.6, §2.6, §3.6).

Conversely, the pipeline **duplicates** the citators if it only produces case-level "A cites B" lists with a coarsened negative/positive judgement: that is precisely the RefLex `Citations` subtab, the KeyCite `Citing References` list with flags/stars, and the Shepard's `Citing Decisions` report with Signals and DoD bars.
